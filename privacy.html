Privacy Notice

Last updated: 6 November 2026

This notice explains what personal data CT Atlas collects from its registered users, why, and how it is protected. CT Atlas is a restricted-access, open-source counter-terrorism situational-awareness tool operated for a defined group of professional users.

What we collect

  • Daily quiz results (retired). The daily quiz was removed on 11 November 2026. The answers recorded during the pilot and the quiz counts in usage statistics were erased the same day.
  • Account credentials. A username and password. Passwords are stored only as a one-way cryptographic hash (SHA-256) and are never stored or logged in plain text.
  • Login records. When you sign in, we record the timestamp, your browser's user-agent string, an approximate country derived from your network location, and a one-way hashed version of your IP address. We never store your raw IP address. This exists solely for security auditing and to detect unauthorised access.
  • Usage counters. We record which CT Atlas features an account uses and how often, and when each workspace is opened.
  • Search history. Since 6 October 2026, each search or request made with an account is recorded with its time and what was searched, so that the administrator can review how CT Atlas is used: the event-list search text and the filters it ran with; the Report Generator selection; the Deep Search question and scope; and the crypto address or transaction looked up (and whether it came from a search, a trace expansion or a monitor check). The outcome of a report request (generated, served from cache, no matching events or failed) and the report title may be added. Only the administrator can view this history; it is kept for 90 days and then deleted automatically.
  • Report Generator. The selected region, topic, period and relevant public-event dataset are sent to Google's Gemini API to generate the analytical report. The generated report is cached in CT Atlas for up to eight hours and is then automatically deleted from active application storage.
  • Deep Search. The analyst's free-text question is sent to Google Gemini for query planning and report generation and is used to query public-source providers. The resulting Deep Search report contains the question, search diagnostics and evidence used. It is cached in CT Atlas for up to four hours and is then automatically deleted from active application storage. The question and its scope are also kept in the administrator's search history for 90 days.
  • Crypto Intelligence. A one-off wallet or transaction lookup is processed through the CT Atlas backend and the relevant public blockchain provider. Each lookup is recorded in the administrator's search history for 90 days (see “Search history”); the results themselves are not kept there. Information that the analyst deliberately saves to the Crypto workspace — such as labels, watchlists, cases, notes, saved paths, monitoring snapshots and alerts — is stored persistently for that account until it is deleted or the workspace is removed. Automated risk indicators are generated from the public data to prompt analyst review, and no decision is made automatically.

What we don't collect

  • No advertising or marketing cookies, and no third-party analytics or tracking scripts.
  • No data is collected from visitors who have not logged in — the login screen itself sets no tracking cookie.

Public event data on the map

The map displays counter-terrorism and organised-crime events compiled from public open-source reporting (news media, and the GDELT and ACLED research projects) about publicly reported incidents. This is not personal data about our registered users, and is retained on a rolling 180-day basis.

Why we process this data

Processing is necessary to operate a restricted-access professional tool: to authenticate registered users, secure accounts against unauthorised access, allocate limited analytical capacity (AI-assisted search and reporting) fairly across users, and let the administrator oversee how the tool is used during the pilot (usage counters and search history).

Who else processes data (service providers and public-source platforms)

  • Cloudflare — hosts the CT Atlas backend API, authentication/session state, rate limiting, usage records and search history, report caches and Crypto workspaces. CT Atlas routes its primary Durable Object state through Cloudflare's European Union jurisdiction.
  • GitHub Pages / GitHub Actions — host and deploy the static application and automation code. User investigation workspaces are not stored in the GitHub repository.
  • Google — the Gemini API is used for AI-assisted analysis. CT Atlas's direct Gemini Interactions calls set store: false. Google News is also one of several public sources used by Deep Search and map collection.
  • Blockchain-data providers — Blockstream Esplora, Etherscan and TronGrid may receive wallet addresses or transaction identifiers necessary to answer Crypto Intelligence requests on supported chains.
  • GDELT, ACLED and other public reporting sources — provide open-source event or reporting data used by CT Atlas.

Retention

Report Generator cache entries expire after eight hours and Deep Search cache entries expire after four hours. CT Atlas schedules backend deletion when those entries expire rather than retaining expired cache entries until a later lookup. Authentication sessions expire after their configured session lifetime and are likewise scheduled for deletion from active application storage.

Analyst-saved Crypto workspaces are persistent by design and remain until they are explicitly deleted or the workspace/account is removed.

The search history behind the usage counters is kept for 90 days: entries older than that are deleted automatically, whole days at a time. The counters themselves (numbers only) are kept for the pilot period.

Social Intelligence (SOCMINT), Dark Web Intelligence, Facial Intelligence and IP Intelligence were withdrawn in November 2026. They can no longer be used, they collect nothing new, and CT Atlas no longer sends anything to the third parties only they relied on. What they had already stored has not been erased. Saved SOCMINT reports are removed once they are 90 days old, and the search-history entries of these workspaces, including the IP addresses and domains looked up in IP Intelligence, follow the 90-day rule above. Their usage counters remain with the others. Dark Web publication records and files, the storage used for temporary face-crop hosting, including any crop still hosted at that moment, and the per-account rate-limit counters of IP Intelligence (numbers only) remain in CT Atlas's private Cloudflare storage, out of reach of the application, until the administrator deletes them. Anything those third parties already received stays subject to their own retention rules.

Login/security records are retained only as long as needed for security auditing; the application keeps a bounded recent login log. Account records persist for the duration of the testing/pilot period they were created for.

Infrastructure providers may maintain independent operational, security, billing, backup or point-in-time-recovery records according to their own service terms and retention policies. Application-level deletion therefore does not necessarily imply immediate erasure of every provider backup copy.

Your rights

You may request access to, correction of, or deletion of your account data at any time by contacting the CT Atlas administrator through your organisation's usual internal channels.

Security and data location

Passwords and your own IP address are never stored in plain text. All connections to CT Atlas use HTTPS encryption. The primary CT Atlas Durable Object used for persistent application state is created through Cloudflare's European Union jurisdiction, constraining that Durable Object's compute and persistent storage to the EU.

Changes to this notice

This notice may be updated as the tool evolves. The date at the top reflects the most recent revision.
