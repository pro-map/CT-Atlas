Privacy Notice

Last updated: 26 September 2026

This notice explains what personal data CT Atlas collects from its registered users, why, and how it is protected. CT Atlas is a restricted-access, open-source counter-terrorism situational-awareness tool operated for a defined group of professional users.

What we collect

  • Daily quiz results. When you answer, we store your account username, quiz date and identifier, first selected answer, correctness and server timestamp, together with the correction shown. Earlier pilot records may contain correctness only. The administrator can view per-account answer counts, correct and incorrect totals and success rates for the selected period. Results are shared between the main site and its mirror. This is an educational self-test, not a secure examination or a measure of professional performance.
  • Account credentials. A username and password. Passwords are stored only as a one-way cryptographic hash (SHA-256) and are never stored or logged in plain text.
  • Login records. When you sign in, we record the timestamp, your browser's user-agent string, an approximate country derived from your network location, and a one-way hashed version of your IP address. We never store your raw IP address. This exists solely for security auditing and to detect unauthorised access.
  • Usage counters. We record which CT Atlas features an account uses and how often. For the ordinary map and event-list text filters, the search text itself is not sent to usage analytics; only a feature-use counter is recorded.
  • Report Generator. The selected region, topic, period and relevant public-event dataset are sent to Google's Gemini API to generate the analytical report. The generated report is cached in CT Atlas for up to eight hours and is then automatically deleted from active application storage.
  • Deep Search. The analyst's free-text question is sent to Google Gemini for query planning and report generation and is used to query public-source providers. The resulting Deep Search report contains the question, search diagnostics and evidence used. It is cached in CT Atlas for up to four hours and is then automatically deleted from active application storage.
  • Crypto Intelligence. A one-off wallet or transaction lookup is processed through the CT Atlas backend and the relevant public blockchain provider. CT Atlas does not maintain an automatic history of every one-off crypto lookup. Information that the analyst deliberately saves to the Crypto workspace — such as labels, watchlists, cases, notes, saved paths, monitoring snapshots and alerts — is stored persistently for that account until it is deleted or the workspace is removed.
  • SOCMINT / Social Intelligence. Investigation inputs (including targets, usernames, keywords, public URLs, platforms, regions, languages and the analytical objective) are processed by the SOCMINT service. The resulting report, including its query context and public-source references, is saved to that user's SOCMINT workspace. CT Atlas keeps no more than 50 reports per user and automatically removes SOCMINT reports older than 90 days.
  • Visual Intelligence. Uploaded images or videos are relayed to the CT Atlas visual-analysis service for processing. CT Atlas does not save uploaded image or video files to its application database. Video processing uses temporary files that are removed after analysis. Infrastructure-level request or security logs may still be generated by the hosting provider. Detected faces can be cut out in your browser; that happens locally and sends nothing. Only when you click SEARCH on a face crop (and confirm), that single crop — re-encoded as a small JPEG without metadata — is hosted for about 10 minutes by the CT Atlas backend so that the free reverse-image search engine(s) you chose can download it, and it is then deleted automatically. Anyone holding the unguessable link can view the crop during that time, and the engine's own retention rules apply to what it downloads.

What we don't collect

  • No advertising or marketing cookies, and no third-party analytics or tracking scripts.
  • No data is collected from visitors who have not logged in — the login screen itself sets no tracking cookie.

Public event data on the map

The map displays counter-terrorism and organised-crime events compiled from public open-source reporting (news media, and the GDELT and ACLED research projects) about publicly reported incidents. This is not personal data about our registered users, and is retained on a rolling 180-day basis.

Why we process this data

Processing is necessary to operate a restricted-access professional tool: to authenticate registered users, secure accounts against unauthorised access, and allocate limited analytical capacity (AI-assisted search and reporting) fairly across users.

Who else processes data (service providers and public-source platforms)

  • Cloudflare — hosts the CT Atlas backend API, authentication/session state, rate limiting, usage records, report caches, Crypto workspaces and SOCMINT workspaces. CT Atlas routes its primary Durable Object state through Cloudflare's European Union jurisdiction.
  • GitHub Pages / GitHub Actions — host and deploy the static application and automation code. User investigation workspaces are not stored in the GitHub repository.
  • Google — Google Cloud Run hosts the CT Atlas SOCMINT agent and visual-analysis service; the Gemini API is used for AI-assisted analysis. CT Atlas's direct Gemini Interactions calls set store: false. Google News is also one of several public sources used by Deep Search and map collection.
  • Public web/search and social platforms — depending on the investigation and configured connectors, SOCMINT may send a search term or public identifier to services such as Brave Search, Bluesky, Mastodon, YouTube, Reddit, Twitch, Flickr, Tumblr, X, the Internet Archive, Odysee, or another explicitly configured public-search provider. These services receive only the requests necessary to return the requested public-source material.
  • Blockchain-data providers — Blockstream Esplora, Etherscan and TronGrid may receive wallet addresses or transaction identifiers necessary to answer Crypto Intelligence requests on supported chains.
  • GDELT, ACLED and other public reporting sources — provide open-source event or reporting data used by CT Atlas.
  • Reverse-image search engines — Yandex, Bing, Google Lens, TinEye and Baidu receive the temporary link of a face crop only when an analyst clicks SEARCH and confirms. Search4faces receives a crop only when the analyst pastes it there. These services are not operated by CT Atlas.

Retention

Report Generator cache entries expire after eight hours and Deep Search cache entries expire after four hours. CT Atlas schedules backend deletion when those entries expire rather than retaining expired cache entries until a later lookup. Authentication sessions expire after their configured session lifetime and are likewise scheduled for deletion from active application storage.

SOCMINT reports are retained for a maximum of 90 days and are also capped at 50 reports per user. Analyst-saved Crypto workspaces are persistent by design and remain until they are explicitly deleted or the workspace/account is removed. Uploaded Visual Intelligence source files are not stored in the CT Atlas application database after processing. A face crop hosted for a reverse-image search is kept for at most 10 minutes (and for a limited number of downloads), then its storage object deletes itself.

Login/security records are retained only as long as needed for security auditing; the application keeps a bounded recent login log. Account records persist for the duration of the testing/pilot period they were created for.

Quiz attempts and aggregate scores are retained during the pilot for learning follow-up and to prevent repeated attempts being counted. They are not currently subject to automatic expiry. You may ask the administrator to delete them. Quiz participation is optional; no quiz result is recorded merely by viewing a question.

Infrastructure providers may maintain independent operational, security, billing, backup or point-in-time-recovery records according to their own service terms and retention policies. Application-level deletion therefore does not necessarily imply immediate erasure of every provider backup copy.

Your rights

You may request access to, correction of, or deletion of your account data at any time by contacting the CT Atlas administrator through your organisation's usual internal channels.

Security and data location

Passwords and IP addresses are never stored in plain text. All connections to CT Atlas use HTTPS encryption. The primary CT Atlas Durable Object used for persistent application state is created through Cloudflare's European Union jurisdiction, constraining that Durable Object's compute and persistent storage to the EU. CT Atlas services deployed on Google Cloud Run are configured by the deployment workflow for the project's selected Google Cloud region.
